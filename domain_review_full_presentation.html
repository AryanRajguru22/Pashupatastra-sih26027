PART I: FORMAL DOMAIN REVIEW (DOMAIN_REVIEW.md)

Executive Summary

Pashupatastra solves a critical real-world operational problem for Indian Railways (IR): how to optimize, schedule, and dynamically re-schedule maintenance blocks (possessions) across busy railway corridors while respecting operational safety, asset conditions, precedence dependencies, and train timetables.

This domain review formalizes the railway domain semantics, identifies limitations in initial baseline assumptions, and establishes concrete recommendations for the Domain Freeze checkpoint before downstream workstreams (Tyagi's Optimizer, Ayush's ML Scorer, Tirth's Simulation, Archit's Frontend, and Aryan's API) build further.

1. Track Modeling & Corridor Representation

Current Assumption: Tracks are identified by simple flat strings: track_id = "UP-1", track_id = "DOWN-1".

Problem Identified: A flat string alone does not convey which corridor, section, or kilometer range the track belongs to. However, introducing a full GIS graph or topological switch network adds unnecessary mathematical complexity to the CP-SAT solver without adding demo value.

Recommendation: Retain track_id: str as the primary key/unique identifier in shared contracts (e.g. "UP-1", "DOWN-1"). In the Domain Data layer, represent tracks with structured metadata (corridor ID, section name, direction, mileage start/end, speed limit, electrification).

Reason & Grounding: In Indian Railways, maintenance blocks are granted per block section along a directional line (Up Main Line, Down Main Line). Preserving a clean string identifier keeps CP-SAT interval constraints simple (no overlapping intervals on the same track), while metadata enables rich UI display on the Frontend dashboard.

Downstream Impact: Optimizer (Tyagi): Zero breaking changes. Frontend (Archit): Groups tracks cleanly by direction. ML Scorer (Ayush): Uses speed limits and density for operational impact scoring.

2. Maintenance Work Types (WorkType)

Current Assumption: Generic categories (RENEWAL, INSPECTION, REPAIR, MAINTENANCE).

Problem Identified: Categories are too generic and fail to reflect Indian Railways maintenance practices (e.g. Track Machine Operations, OHE Power Blocks, S&T Interlocking). They do not capture why certain tasks require heavy machinery or complete electrical isolation.

Recommendation: Standardize the WorkType enum into 6 concrete railway maintenance categories:

WorkType Description Typical Duration Machinery / Resource Note Priority Range
TRACK_RENEWAL Complete Track Renewal (CTR), Through Sleeper Renewal (TSR), Rail Renewal (TRR) 180 – 360 min Heavy possession (BCM, PQRS, T-28), total track closure 0.85 – 0.95
BALLAST_TAMPING Track packing and alignment using Tie-Tamping machines (CSM, Duomatic) 90 – 180 min Requires safety headway buffer after track renewal 0.70 – 0.85
OHE_MAINTENANCE Overhead Equipment inspection, contact wire replacement, insulator washing 60 – 150 min Requires Electrical Power Block (OHE isolation); Tower Wagon 0.65 – 0.80
SIGNALLING_INTERLOCKING Point machine overhaul, track circuit testing, axle counter maintenance 45 – 120 min Affects interlocking & station approaches; Signal Gang 0.60 – 0.75
ROUTINE_INSPECTION Ultrasonic Flaw Detection (USFD), foot patrol, geometry recording 30 – 90 min Minimal disruption; manual trolley or inspection unit 0.35 – 0.50
EMERGENCY_REPAIR Rail fracture, weld failure, OHE wire parting, point failure 60 – 180 min Unscheduled, pre-emptive, highest urgency score 0.95 – 0.99

3. Disruption Types (DisruptionType)

We define 4 standardized, deterministic disruption types for the PLAN → DISRUPT → RECOVER demonstration:

1. TRACK_UNAVAILABLE (Scenario 1 — Track Blockage)
Description: A track segment becomes unusable from time $t_1$ to $t_2$ (e.g. detected rail defect, obstacle, speed restriction).
Impact: Blocks scheduled on that track during that interval become AFFECTED and must be rescheduled to another track or moved in time.

2. EMERGENCY_WORK (Scenario 2 — Critical Unscheduled Work Insertion)
Description: An urgent emergency repair block (e.g. rail fracture) appears at time $t$ with maximum priority (0.99).
Impact: CP-SAT must schedule the emergency block immediately, shifting or dropping lower-priority routine blocks while protecting already committed blocks.

3. POSSESSION_CURTAILMENT (Scenario 3 — Time Window Shortened)
Description: Due to delayed express trains, an available possession window (e.g. 08:00–12:00) is curtailed to (09:30–11:30).
Impact: Maintenance tasks that no longer fit must be compressed, rescheduled to later slots, or marked unscheduled with clear reason codes.

4. ASSET_BREAKDOWN (Scenario 4 — Machine / Equipment Failure)
Description: A heavy track machine (e.g. BCM) breaks down mid-block, extending work duration or halting all tasks sharing that machine group.

4. Resource & Machinery Constraints

Current Assumption: Complex multi-resource models (crew_id, crew_type, equipment_id, crew_capacity).

Problem Identified: Full resource leveling and human crew rostering turns the problem into a 2-stage multi-mode RCPSP, risking slow solve times (>10s) and artificial infeasibility during a live hackathon demo.

Recommendation: Do NOT model individual human workers in Phase 1. Model shared heavy machines via mutual_exclusion_group: Optional[str] (e.g. BCM_MACHINE_01, CSM_TAMPER_01, TOWER_WAGON_01). The CP-SAT solver enforces: $$\text{Interval}(\text{Block}_1) \cap \text{Interval}(\text{Block}_2) = \emptyset \quad \forall \text{ blocks sharing same machine group}$$ This guarantees that the single available BCM machine is not scheduled in two places simultaneously, without bloating the solver.

5. Train Timetable & Operational Window Representation

Current Assumption: train_timetable field exists in the contract but lacks clear solver constraints.

Recommendation: Model train traffic as Discrete Available Possession Windows per track over the 24-hour horizon:

Window ID Track Window Type Clock Time Minute Timeline Allocated Maintenance Work
POS-UP1-NIGHT UP-1 NIGHT_TRAFFIC_BLOCK 00:30 – 05:00 0030 – 0300 (270 min) Heavy Track Renewal (CTR) & Ballast Tamping
POS-UP1-MIDDAY UP-1 MIDDAY_MAINTENANCE 11:30 – 14:30 0690 – 0870 (180 min) USFD Inspection & OHE Isolations
POS-UP1-EVENING UP-1 EVENING_OFF_PEAK 21:00 – 23:30 1260 – 1410 (150 min) Point Machines & Signalling Interlocking
POS-DN1-NIGHT DOWN-1 NIGHT_TRAFFIC_BLOCK 00:30 – 05:00 0030 – 0300 (270 min) Heavy Track Renewal (CTR) & Ballast Tamping
POS-DN1-MIDDAY DOWN-1 MIDDAY_MAINTENANCE 11:30 – 14:30 0690 – 0870 (180 min) USFD Inspection & OHE Isolations
POS-DN1-EVENING DOWN-1 EVENING_OFF_PEAK 21:00 – 23:30 1260 – 1410 (150 min) Point Machines & Signalling Interlocking

Solver Constraint: Every scheduled block $[s_i, e_i]$ on track $T$ must lie strictly within an available window:

$$\exists W \in \text{PossessionWindows}(T) \quad \text{such that} \quad W.\text{start} \le s_i \quad \text{and} \quad e_i \le W.\text{end}$$

6. Precedence Dependencies & Sequential Chains

Maintenance operations follow strict sequential engineering protocols:

$$\text{Track Renewal (CTR)} \xrightarrow{\text{precedes}} \text{Deep Ballast Screening (BCM)} \xrightarrow{\text{precedes}} \text{Tie Tamping (CSM)} \xrightarrow{\text{precedes}} \text{Speed Normalization}$$

If Block $B$ depends on Block $A$ (dependencies: ["BLK-A"]), CP-SAT enforces: $\text{start\_time}(B) \ge \text{end\_time}(A) + \text{buffer}$.

7. Summary Checklist for Domain Freeze

  • Track Modeling: String track_id preserved for solver; metadata dictionary available for UI.
  • Work Types: 6 standardized Indian Railways categories agreed.
  • Disruption Types: 4 deterministic disruption types defined.
  • Resources: Simplified to mutual_exclusion_group for heavy machines.
  • Train Timetable: Modeled as discrete PossessionWindow intervals.
  • Synthetic Generator: Seed-driven implementation ready for downstream integration.
